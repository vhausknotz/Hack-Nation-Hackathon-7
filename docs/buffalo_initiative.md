# Who the sponsor is: the Buffalo Initiative

Background research on the challenge co-sponsor (with OpenAI), gathered 2026-10-03 from [buffaloinitiative.org](https://buffaloinitiative.org/) and other public sources. Read it to understand what the people judging this challenge care about.

## In short

- **What:** a mission-driven program of **Renaissance Philanthropy**, building "a scalable, investable platform for ultra-rare therapeutic development." Founded March 2024. Tagline: *Patient-Driven Cures*.
- **Mission:** "turn patient-led breakthroughs into approved medicines at scale."
- **Central premise:** **"The Science Is Ready. The System Is Not."** Families have organized research and launched early programs. "What they lacked was not commitment or science but infrastructure, capital and therapeutic development expertise."
- **Focus:** ultra-rare diseases (fewer than 3,000 US patients) that are **monogenic**, have well-understood causes and clear therapeutic hypotheses, and have **patient-led programs already moving toward the clinic**. Their roots are in rare pediatric brain diseases; their tracker also covers neuromuscular, metabolic, ophthalmology and immunology.
- **Founder:** **Sunitha Malepati**, an attorney with a background in private investment and nonprofit law, and the parent of a child with a rare neurogenetic disorder. Also Vice President of the **CACNA1A Foundation** and on the governing board of **COMBINEDBrain**.
- **The name:** "Buffalo turn and charge straight into it—together—spending less time in the storm."
- **Role they claim:** connective tissue. "Buffalo is not a replacement for academia, pharma, or patient groups"; it bridges discovery, development and delivery.

## Their model: four components (from their website)

| Component | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Pipeline** | Continuously identify high-quality, patient-led programs |
| **Accelerator** | Expert support from preclinical to patients (toxicology studies, FDA strategy, manufacturing) |
| **Open Source** | Shared playbooks so every program makes the next one faster |
| **Blended Capital** | Financing on biological timelines, not investor exit pressure. Other sources cite a $50M, 10-year blended fund and a "Cure Chaser Fund" of $2–8M per patient organization to cross the "valley of death" |

Outcomes they target: competitive financial returns, life-changing therapies, and a model that can be repeated across hundreds of diseases.

## Their Therapy Tracker (a possible data source)

[buffaloinitiative.org/pipeline](https://buffaloinitiative.org/pipeline/) is a public, filterable list of **patient-led therapeutic programs**. It had 60+ entries across 7+ pages when checked.

- **Fields:** disease, program name, therapeutic modality (biologic, small molecule repurposed or novel, nucleic acid/RNA/ASO, gene therapy, gene editing), therapeutic area, development stage (target validation, proof of concept, toxicology, Phase I to III) and the patient organization behind it.
- **Examples:**
  - CACNA1A-related: upregulating ASO, and drug repurposing (CACNA1A Foundation)
  - Angelman syndrome: GTX-102 ASO in Phase III, and gene therapies (FAST)
  - 5p- syndrome: ASO and repurposing screen (Cri du Chat Research Foundation)
  - CASK disorder: gene therapy (Cure CASK)
  - ADSS1 myopathy: Phase I (Cure ADSSL1)
  - Alternating hemiplegia: prime editing (RARE Hope)

**Why it matters to us:** these programs are exactly the "existing work" Maria needs to find. Bringing Buffalo's own tracker into the atlas as program and asset nodes connects it to genes, mechanisms and neighboring diseases. It answers questions like "which *other* diseases could this modality plausibly serve?" (Priya) and "which program in my neighborhood is furthest along?" (Maria). We must cite the tracker as the source and check its terms before using the data.

## Related: COMBINEDBrain

A nonprofit consortium of rare genetic neurodevelopmental-disorder patient groups. Sources count anywhere from 60 to 90+ groups. Members include the SLC6A1, SCN2A, SETBP1, FOXG1, SHANK3 and Glut1 groups. They pool natural history efforts and run a shared **biorepository** (blood and urine samples available to researchers).

This is a real example of the "shared asset a tiny group can join instead of rebuilding" that the brief asks us to surface.

## What this means for our build

1. **Pick a monogenic, pediatric neurogenetic slice.** That is Buffalo's home turf, and the judges will recognize good versus naive output there.
2. **The atlas is the discovery layer for Buffalo's own model.** Their thesis is shared playbooks and infrastructure across patient-led programs. Our product finds *which* infrastructure, *which* neighbors and *which* next step, with evidence.
3. **Connect their Therapy Tracker.** Their real programs appear inside the graph, matched to the diseases they could also help.
4. **Buffalo is also a funder.** A view of "where each community stands on the road to treatment, and what is missing" serves them directly (the Priya persona), not just Maria.
5. **Use their language:** patient-led, the science is ready, valley of death, shared playbooks, every program makes the next one faster.
